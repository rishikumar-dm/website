The problem

Ranking for your own name is not marketing

When a patient types the practice name into Google, they have already chosen you. Showing up is not a win — it is the minimum. Yet that is where most of the practice's search visibility was sitting.

Brand searches are a rear-view mirror. They measure a reputation already built through years of chairside work and word of mouth. They grow when the practice grows — they do not cause it.

Of the ten procedures on the brief, almost none were producing search visibility at all. Implants, crowns, root canals, veneers, bridges, extractions, fillings, scaling, clear aligners — the treatments that actually pay for a practice. A patient searching "dental implants cost duncanville" was landing on somebody else, and nobody knew it was happening.

The only searches that add new patients are the ones made by people who have never heard of you. Every single one of those is a procedure search. So that is what we went and took.

The strategy

A silo per procedure, with the city inside the URL

Each of the ten procedures got its own directory, and inside it a page written for the exact way local patients search — the procedure plus both city names. Not one services page with ten paragraphs. Ten silos, each capable of ranking on its own.

The content layer

Then we took the modifiers

Ranking for "dental implants duncanville" wins the patient who already knows what she wants. Most patients do not. They search around the decision first — what it costs, what the alternative is, what actually happens, whether it is worth it. Every one of those is a separate search, and each was built as its own page.

Cost. Benefits. Alternatives. Procedure. Versus. Five modifiers, ten procedures, two cities. That is the whole content plan — and it is why the practice appears repeatedly for a patient researching the same treatment over three weeks.

Off-site

We did not only rank the website

A dental practice's own domain can realistically hold one or two spots on a search. The rest of page one belongs to somebody — so we made sure some of it belonged to the practice anyway.

  • A branded Quora Space, ranking on page oneOriginal procedure articles published to the practice's own Quora space earned first-page positions for "dental scaling cost duncanville" and "dental bridges duncanville" — alongside the website, not instead of it.
  • Medium articles holding positionA ClearCorrect article on Medium took a first-page slot on "clear correct benefits duncanville," giving the practice three of the visible results on that search.
  • The point is coverage, not backlinksEvery slot on page one belonging to the practice is a slot that does not belong to the dentist down the road. Syndication is how a single practice occupies more of a page than its domain authority should allow.

Questions

Dental SEO, answered plainly

How long before a dental practice sees results?

Google Business Profile improvements and quick technical wins typically move within 30 to 60 days. Procedure keywords in a competitive metro like Dallas take three to six months to reach page one, and the long-tail modifier pages usually land first because there is less competition on them.

How do you calculate what a call is worth?

Take your booking rate, your show rate, and your average first-year patient value, and multiply through. Most practices have never done it, which is why marketing feels like a cost rather than an investment. The calculator above starts at 45% booking, 80% show and $1,200 per patient. Plug in your own numbers — practices with implants and crowns in the mix usually land higher.

Can one page rank in two cities?

Yes, and this engagement is the proof — the same pages carried Dallas and Duncanville in the URL, title and heading, and ranked for both. It works when the cities are genuinely adjacent and you actually serve patients in each. It does not work as a way to fake a presence in a city you have no office in.

Should we publish our prices?

We recommend it. The featured snippet in this case study was won by publishing an actual cost range. Patients search cost more than almost anything else, and practices that refuse to answer hand the position to whoever will.

Does Google Images traffic actually matter for a dentist?

It matters more than most practices expect, because almost nobody competes there. Custom branded graphics on every article gave this practice multiple tiles on the first screen of image results for procedure searches — visibility bought with design work rather than link budget.

Do you work with multi-location practices?

Yes. Multi-location work needs a distinct Business Profile strategy per office plus a site architecture that does not have the locations competing with each other, which is exactly the structure used here.
