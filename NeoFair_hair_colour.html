THE SERVICE, EXPLAINED

Colour that works with your starting shade

Hair colour changes more than the shade. It can add depth to a haircut, brighten selected sections or blend new grey growth into the lengths. An all-over colour creates one visual effect; fine highlights create another. The relationship between the base shade, lighter pieces and their placement determines how natural, soft or striking the result appears.

NeoFair’s hair-colouring range includes root touch-ups, global colour, partial and global highlights, face framing, balayage, ombre and corrective work. Natural browns and softer accents can suit an everyday look, while stronger contrast and fashion shades create a more visible change. Previous colour and the condition of the hair influence the colour plan.

01 / EXPLORE THE SERVICES

Different techniques. Different colour effects.

Colour placement is as important as the shade. Root work focuses on new growth; global colour changes the overall tone; highlights and painted techniques add contrast through selected sections. Each creates a different pattern as the hair moves.

01

Root colour & grey coverage

A root touch-up concentrates colour on the new growth nearest the scalp. It restores continuity between the roots and previously coloured lengths, particularly where grey hair is visible along the parting or hairline. T-line work focuses on a smaller visible area. Refreshing faded lengths is a separate colour task, so root coverage can maintain an established shade without treating the entire head in the same way each time.

02

Global colour

Global hair colour applies a selected shade across the hair for an overall refresh or change. A natural shade can create a more uniform appearance, while a fashion shade adds a stronger colour statement. Dark hair does not always reach a lighter target shade in one colouring step; some changes need pre-lightening. Existing colour also affects the result, so the finished tone depends on the starting base as well as the shade selected.

03

Highlights & face framing

Highlights add lighter sections among the base colour, creating dimension rather than one continuous shade. Fine sections produce a softer blend; bolder placement creates more contrast. NeoFair’s range includes single streaks, partial highlights, face-framing highlights and global highlights. Colour around the front can brighten the overall look, while highlights through the lengths become more visible in waves, layers and tied-up styles.

04

Balayage & ombre

Balayage is a painting technique that places lightness selectively through the hair. Its softly blended transitions can leave more of the natural depth near the roots. Ombre describes a graduated colour effect, usually moving from darker roots to visibly lighter ends. Balayage can create subtle ribbons; ombre makes the change through the length more pronounced. Both can be adapted through tone and placement, from gentle brown variation to a stronger contrast.

05

Corrective work & bond-care options

Colour correction addresses a specific problem such as uneven bands, unwanted warmth or an old shade that no longer suits the intended look. It can involve balancing tone, adjusting depth or lifting selected sections in stages. A major correction may take more than one appointment. Bond-care options can accompany suitable colour services to support the condition of processed hair, while a toner refines the visible shade after lightening.

THE SERVICE EXPERIENCE

From the first step to the final detail.

01

Starting colour

Natural shade, grey growth and previous colour form the base for the new result. The history of bleach, dye or henna affects what can be achieved.

02

Placement and tone

The technique establishes where the colour sits. Tone controls whether the finish appears warmer, cooler or more neutral, and the amount of contrast changes its impact.

03

Refinement

Toning, rinsing, conditioning and styling complete the colour process as appropriate to the service. The finished style reveals how the shades blend and move together.

After the appointment

Colour maintenance includes caring for the lengths and refreshing the areas that change with growth or fading. Gentle cleansing, conditioning and sensible use of heat help maintain the feel of coloured hair. Root coverage, toner refreshes and a full colour appointment address different stages of maintenance.

IN DETAIL

Understand the service before you choose.

How the starting hair changes the colour plan

Natural black or deep brown hair, previously coloured hair and hair with grey growth are different starting points. Grey coverage relies on a shade that blends the new roots with the existing lengths. Brightening dark hair often requires lightening before the desired tone can be seen. Old dye, especially very dark permanent colour, can affect how evenly the hair lifts. If a reference shows a soft caramel brown, its apparent warmth may come from both the lighter sections and the untouched dark base. That is why two people can request the same shade photograph and need different processes to approach it.

Placement: the detail behind the finished look

A partial highlight concentrates lighter strands in selected areas, while a fuller highlight distributes brightness across more of the head. Face-framing highlights bring attention to the front of the haircut and can remain visible when the hair is tied back. Balayage places lightness with a painted, blended effect; ombre places a visible progression from deeper roots to lighter mid-lengths and ends. Tone then refines the colour after the placement is complete. Warmer caramel and copper tones read differently from cooler brown or ash tones against dark Indian hair, and daylight often reveals warmth that indoor light hides.

What a colour appointment includes and how it grows out

A colour service may involve preparation, sectioning, application, processing, rinsing, toning or conditioning, followed by a finish that shows the colour in movement. Not every service includes every step: a root touch-up is a different appointment from pre-lightening plus fashion colour. As hair grows, root coverage becomes visibly separate from the coloured lengths and needs a refresh. Painted brightness can retain more natural depth near the roots, but the lighter sections still need care and may shift tone with washing, sun and heat. Colour correction is more complex when the hair has uneven bands or several previous colours; it can require staged work rather than one dramatic appointment.

FIND YOUR FIT

Two ways to approach your visit.

For a subtle change

A root refresh, T-line, a few face-framing pieces or soft highlights can make a visible difference while keeping the overall style familiar.

For a major change

Global colour, noticeable balayage, ombre or corrective work changes a larger part of the hair. The scope and quote depend on length, density, old colour and the number of processes needed.

What shapes the quote

Length, density, starting shade and technique affect product use and time. Pre-lightening, toning, bond care and corrective stages can change the scope considerably. The final quote should describe the actual service rather than a photograph alone.

03 / SKILL IN THE DETAILS

Technique makes the difference.

Colour work depends on controlled placement and a clear understanding of the starting hair. Section size, colour depth and blending all affect the finish. The colourist’s role is to bring these details together so the lighter pieces, base shade and haircut feel connected.

05 / BEFORE YOU DECIDE

Useful answers.

Clear explanations of the differences between services, finishes and care.

What is the difference between balayage and highlights?+

Balayage uses painted placement for a blended effect. Highlights is the broader term for lighter sections within the hair, often created with more structured sectioning. The final effect depends on placement, contrast and tone.

Does ombre always mean blonde ends?+

No. Ombre describes the transition between deeper and lighter colour. Brown, caramel or other shades can create that gradient; blonde is one possible finish.

Can a root touch-up refresh the whole colour?+

A root touch-up covers new growth. Faded lengths or a change in tone may need a separate refresh, so the two services have different purposes.

Can very dark coloured hair become much lighter in one visit?+

A major change may need staged work, especially when old dye is present. The existing colour and hair condition determine how much lightening is appropriate in one appointment.

Do I need a full-head colour to cover grey roots?+

Usually no. A root touch-up targets new growth; a T-line focuses on selected visible areas. Faded lengths or a new overall tone may require an additional service.

Is balayage always low maintenance?+

Leaving depth at the root can soften visible regrowth, but lighter sections still need suitable washing, conditioning and sometimes a tone refresh.

Why does a colour correction cost more than a regular colour?+

Correction can involve extra stages to manage old dye, uneven tone or bands. Time, product use, hair length and the work required affect the quote.
